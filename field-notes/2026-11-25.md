# Quoting 3,200 sponges in cases of 24

A janitorial bid from the Parish of Caddo asks for a case price on cellulose sponges. The estimate is in pieces. One quantity belongs in the demand column and another belongs in the shipping plan. [Public bid, IFB 26-18, bid form p. 2](https://caddo.gov/event/ifb-26-18-janitorial-supplies-trash-liners-paper-products-disposable-goods/)

| Check | Result |
| --- | ---: |
| Requested pieces | 3,200 |
| Complete cases that fit within demand | 133 × 24 = 3,192 pieces |
| Pieces still needed | 8 |
| Exact case equivalents | 133⅓ |
| Shipment if only whole cases are available | 134 cases = 3,216 pieces |

The extra 16 pieces matter when extending the price. At an illustrative $30 per case, exact demand extends to $4,000. Shipping 134 whole cases extends to $4,020. The $20 is the difference between those assumptions. The example uses no supplier price and makes no claim about an awarded bid.

To reproduce the check in a spreadsheet, put `3200` in A2, `24` in B2 and your case price in C2. Use `=A2/B2*C2` for the exact extension and `=ROUNDUP(A2/B2,0)*C2` for the whole-case extension. Keep both amounts visible until the estimator confirms the pricing basis against the issuer’s instructions and addenda.
